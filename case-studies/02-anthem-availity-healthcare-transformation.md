# Case Study: Scaling Delivery Across 3–5 Agile Teams for Anthem & Availity

**Role:** Product Manager / Technical Product Owner
**Team:** 3–5 Agile teams, 40+ engineers, 20+ business & technology stakeholders

## Context

Anthem and Availity's healthcare transformation program spanned multiple enterprise platforms, delivered by several Agile teams working in parallel. The challenge wasn't any single feature — it was keeping delivery coherent and predictable at that scale.

## Problem

- Coordinating 3–5 teams meant constant risk of dependency collisions and duplicated or conflicting work.
- 20+ stakeholders across business and technology needed a consistent, trustworthy view of progress.
- Quality had been inconsistent — production defects were eating into delivery velocity.

## My Approach

- Owned backlog prioritization, sprint planning, and release planning across the program, not just for a single team.
- Ran RAID (Risks, Assumptions, Issues, Dependencies) management as an active discipline rather than a static log — surfacing cross-team dependencies before they became blockers.
- Worked closely with Engineering, QA, and Operations on post-release stabilization, treating the sprint after a release as seriously as the sprint before it.
- Built a consistent stakeholder communication cadence so 20+ stakeholders got signal, not noise.

## Outcome

- Delivered **15+ major releases** while coordinating the full stakeholder group.
- **Reduced production defects by 30%.**
- Achieved **95% sprint commitment adherence** — a proxy for how trustworthy the roadmap had become to stakeholders.

## What this taught me about product work

At multi-team scale, the PM's real job shifts from "what should we build" to "what will collide with what, and when." RAID management and stabilization discipline did more for delivery predictability here than any single feature decision.
